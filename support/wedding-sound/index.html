How to Choose the Right Wedding Sound System

September 06, 2026 weddings planning speakers microphones

Your wedding day sound needs to cover three very different moments — the ceremony, cocktail hour, and reception — and each one has its own requirements for speakers, microphones, and backup gear. Here’s how to plan it properly so nothing gets left to chance.

📍 Ceremony Sound

The ceremony is the most unforgiving part of the day for audio — there’s no re-take if a mic cuts out during the vows.

  • Scout the location first. Indoor or outdoor, note the available power source and how accessible it is for cable runs.
  • Choose 1–2 speakers based on your guest count and whether the space is open-air or enclosed.
  • Plan your microphones:
    • A lapel mic for the officiant
    • A handheld mic as backup or for readings
    • A mic for the couple, if you want your vows amplified
  • Test the wireless transmitter and receiver ahead of time, and add windscreens to any mic used outdoors.
  • Set up a playback system (laptop or phone with your playlist ready) with an AUX or Bluetooth adapter, and confirm music cues with whoever is running sound.
  • Schedule a sound check before guests arrive, and pack backup batteries, cables, and — if possible — a backup speaker or mic.

🍸 Cocktail Hour Sound

Cocktail hour is about ambiance, not volume.

  • One speaker for ambient music is usually enough.
  • Have a preloaded playlist or a DJ connection ready to go.
  • Use a battery-powered speaker if there’s no convenient power source.
  • Pre-set volume to low/medium — this is background music, not a dance floor.

🥂 Reception Sound

This is where things get technical, with a DJ or band, toasts, and dancing all sharing the same system.

  • Confirm coordination with your DJ or band ahead of time, and have a backup system in case of failure.
  • Set up a dedicated mic and stand for toasts.
  • Confirm dance floor speaker positioning so sound is even across the space.
  • Sync lighting with the sound system if it’s part of your package.
  • Test and charge the MC mic before guests sit down for dinner.

🔌 Tech & Logistics

The details that keep everything running smoothly:

  • Pack power cords, surge protectors, and extension cables.
  • Use gaffer tape or cable covers to secure any wires guests might walk over.
  • Confirm load-in/setup time and breakdown time with your planner or venue.
  • Have a rain contingency plan — tents, speaker covers, etc. — for outdoor setups.
  • Assign an on-site tech if you’re booking a full-service package.
  • Send your insurance certificate to the venue in advance if it’s required.
